Auditing a Private Practice

Key Principles of Auditing
Independence: Auditors should be impartial and not influenced by the process being audited.
Objectivity: Findings should be based on evidence and facts.
Competence: Auditors must have the necessary skills and knowledge to perform the audit effectively.
Confidentiality: Sensitive information should be handled appropriately to protect the organization’s interests.
Common Audit Techniques
Interviews: Speaking with employees to understand processes and identify issues.
Document Review: Examining records, reports, and other documents to verify compliance.
Observation: Watching processes in action to see if they conform to documented procedures.
Sampling: Reviewing a sample of transactions or records to assess the overall process.

Auditing a Private Practice


Benefits of Auditing
Improved Processes: Identifies inefficiencies and areas for improvement.
Compliance: Ensures adherence to standards and regulations.
Risk Management: Detects and mitigates risks early.
Enhanced Reputation: Demonstrates a commitment to quality and continuous improvement.
Challenges in Auditing
Resistance to Change: Employees may be resistant to findings that suggest changes.
Bias: Ensuring the audit remains objective can be difficult, especially in internal audits.
Resource Allocation: Conducting thorough audits requires time and resources.
Coaching: If another employee answers questions for the person being interviewed, this can skew findings.

Plan

PHC Execution: PHC Plan & Plan Meeting
To complete a PHC: Plan, open the PHC Packet that you copied, named, and stored in the customer folder.  The first tab in the PHC Packet is the PHC Plan.

For a standard 2 day PHC, there are multiple ways to split the schedule so you can most efficiently gather all the information you need.   You will likely need to front-load your work on the first day because you will need to reserve some of your second day for report writing and the final presentation.  If you are completing all 7 domains, you may want to plan to finish 5 of the 7 domains on day 1 and leave the remainder for day 2.

Schedule the Presentation for the final hour of Day 2 → Communicate this to the customer. 
Interview & Observe are done with staff → Inform the Practice Owner of which staff will be required for which sections.
Review is done without staff, but we need specific documentation and VPN access → Inform the Practice Owner of the documents needed and/or how to VPN successfully.
Plan to complete Interview and Observe for each category at the same time so you don’t have to keep interrupting the customer’s day.  Because there are two different parties involved (Management & Biller/Admin), you may want to consider the following: 
Complete ALL of the Management Interview for the day’s domains first and then move to the Biller/Admin to complete the remainder of Interview & Observe.OR 
You can do Interview & Observe for 1 Domain then Review that domain alone while the customer rep takes a break.OR
You can do Interview & Observe for All scheduled domains for the day then complete all Review back to back while alone. 

If you have more than 2 days, you can spread the domains out, but it is still recommended to front load the first day.  Then you can either leave space at the end of the second and third day to report write, or do all investigation on days 1-2 and report write the entire 3rd day.

These things all need to be determined with the customer.  Communicate clearly about the involvement we will need from their team so they can pick 2+ days that allow for their staff to be focused on the PHC requirements.  Offer the options above and choose the one that is the most helpful for them to complete their work requirements.

Finalize the PHC: Plan with the specific dates and times for each interview, including who is required for each interview.  If you do NOT want someone involved in an interview, communicate that now.  The interviews will be purer without management involved in Biller/Admin interviews.

Schedule the presentation for the last day and make sure everyone knows they should be there!

PHC Execution: Interview
To complete the actual PHC, move to Tab 2.

I mentioned previously that you can split this up however you’d like to alleviate the burden on the customer’s staff and ensure the best use of everyone’s time.

From the beginning of the Tab 2, complete all the Interview Questions according to your schedule.  The tab you are in represents a single domain, the questions are divided by Category, by PHC type (Interview, Observe, or Review), and by Interviewee (Management vs. Admin/Biller).

Listen to the answer, summarize it back to them in words (as appropriate), and then write your summary into the “Answer:” field.  Finish all the questions as scheduled, moving to Observe and back to Interview as planned.

If you have planned to do all the Interview for the scheduled domains back to back or are moving from one domain Interview & Observe to the next domains Interview & Observe, just move to Tab 3 to advance to the next Domain.

NOTE: You do not have to complete the domains in the order in which they exist.  Feel free to move the tabs around as you are preparing for completion if a different order is best for the practice (example: maybe they complete all E&B at the end of the business day - move it to slot 4 so it’s at the end of day 1).

Report

PHC Execution: Report
You may never feel like you are DONE with a PHC, especially if there is a LOT of discovery and findings.  In this case, consider yourself finished if you either have finished with all of the tasks in the Domain tabs of the PHC Packet OR have found clear discoveries in each section that, if fixed, would bring large value to the customer.  Because PHCs are designed to the recurrent, you do NOT need to fix all the issue in a single year. 

Recurrent PHCs are meant to do 2 things: 1) See if the corrective actions following the previous year’s PHC were effective, and 2) drill down into domains that weren’t focused on in the previous year for lack of time.  
Hopefully this frees you to move through the domains at a slightly faster clip.

Once you are DONE according to the definition above, move to report writing.  You will already have much of the objective evidence already in the report, so now it is a matter of completing the following for each Domain: 

Title Page - Fill in the Name of Practice, PS Rep, and Date of the Presentation.
Introduction (Page 4-6) - Add information to these sections about the customer and the PHC as it occurred. 
For Each Domain - 
Interview Details: Date & Time, Interviewee (Names), and Summary (brief description on how the interviews were conducted and if they went as expected)
Overview: Write a description for each of the following
The process as described by Management
The process as it actually functions
The technology and documents needed to run the process (This should come from the lists on the Review slide)
Findings and Recommendations: 
Findings - “red flags” for each domain category are listed in each “Findings” section.  Remove all that don’t apply. 
Recommendations - Using the “Findings” suggest corrective actions that are needed to “cure” each finding.  1 recommendation may cure multiple findings, so be sure to reference the applicable finding next to the recommendation.
Next Steps: Implementation - 
Insert drop downs for the services you want to sell based on the recommendations from each section.  Once they are entered, you can copy and paste as values so the text wraps and isn’t cut off. 
Put them in order of importance for the practice.
Conclusion - Write a conclusion for the practice, giving them on overall “health grade”, what they are doing well, and where they should focus first to see great improvement.

Prepare Options
Prioritize your findings in the order of impact on the practice. 
Present the provider with 3 tiers of implementation packages:
The “Can’t Live Without”
The “Cherry on Top”
The “Complete Package”
Make recommendations in order of severity.

Smaller projects for implementation are easier to deliver. Offer them three tiers of options for implementation sales.
Can’t Live Without - the most important finding and implementation recommendation that you have to offer
Cherry on Top - your most important, and the next most important that just makes the package that much sweeter
Complete Package - All findings and recommendations
