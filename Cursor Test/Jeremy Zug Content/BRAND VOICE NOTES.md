# Tone

## 12 Elements of Tone

**Word Length**: short words are simple and direct, while longer ones suggest sophistication and nuance. Shorter words tend to be punchier and harder, which is great if you want your tone to be bold, while longer words can give a softer, more relaxed effect. So if you want the character of your tone to be pensive and gentle, then it’s good to incorporate longer words into your content.	  
**Sentence Length**: 14 Words Average. No run-on sentences. Avoid too many adjective strings. Read the sentence out loud and see if it feels natural, or could use a pause.   
**Tempo**: Pain point, followed by succinct fact, followed by commentary. End with the possibilities. 5 sentence maximum paragraph length.   
**Pronouns**: We, Practice Solutions.  providers/you, clinicians, healthcare providers,   
	For example, when writing about your company, you can use the first person “we” or third person “Acrolinx.” The first person is more immediate, positioning yourself as a group of people, while the third person is much more detached and abstract, with less clarity about who’s speaking. When writing for an audience, you can use the second person “you” or the third person “customers,” or “suppliers.” The second person is direct and engaging, while the third is more distanced. We’ve written this blog in first and second person, which makes it very clear we’re addressing you directly.  
**Conciseness**: Term; definition.   
	  
**Jargon and Obscure Words**: 

* Jargon is necessary in our field, but should be defined in relatable terms.  
* Obscure Words: 1-2 per article. Bring the “smart”.  
  * No List  
    * Labyrinth  
    * Byzantine  
    * Greek mythology  
    * Cornerstone  
    * Unless order matters, don’t introduce points with “first”, “second,” etc.  
    * Let’s face it;

**Buzzwords and Cliches**:

* Buzzwords: no  
* Cliches: Yes, in the right context  
  * Yes: Can’t judge a book by its cover, time heals all wounds,   
    * Start with a cliche, end with a different perspective on the cliche that is applicable to the topic  
  * No: Crucial, vital, critical, essential with a full stop. Give context to why something is crucial, vital, critical, or essential.

**Contractions** Allowed  
**Coloquialisms**: Verify that any slang terms used do not have unintended meanings. Be an Expert chatting to a non-expert  
**Mistakes and Rulebreaking**: Grammar can be departed from if it matches a natural speech pattern.  
**Emotionally Aware and Inclusive**: YES\! 

* Emotions we want providers to feel: calm, trust, understanding, confidence, peace, conviction, directionality  
* Emotions providers are feeling: frustration, anger, despair, hopelessness, lost, tired, confused
