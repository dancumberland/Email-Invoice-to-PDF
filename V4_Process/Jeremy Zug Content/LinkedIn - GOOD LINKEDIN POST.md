TRICARE goes AWOL. 

The insurance that’s supposed to cover military families but somehow ends up ghosting providers like a bad first date.

If you haven’t heard, the transition to a new contractor has been seamless—in the same way that stepping on a rake is seamless. 

Thousands of claims are unpaid, calls to the new administrator are met with hold music purgatory, and providers are left wondering if they should start bartering for services.

Insurance transitions are like hurricanes—you can’t stop them, but you can board up the windows. 

Here’s how private practice owners can protect their cash flow when payers decide to take an unannounced sabbatical:

✅ Diversify payer mix – If 80% of your revenue comes from one insurance company, you’re one update away from a financial meltdown. Spread out your risk.

✅ Verify eligibility early and often – Just because a patient had coverage doesn’t mean they have coverage. Run eligibility checks like your revenue depends on it (because it does).

✅ Stockpile reserves – Build a buffer. If insurance hits pause, your payroll shouldn’t. Aim for at least three months of operating expenses in reserves.

✅ Follow up aggressively – Your claims are competing for attention with everyone else’s claims. Get in line early, stay on the phone, escalate when needed. Persistence pays (literally).

✅ Educate your clients – Patients need to know that their coverage is changing and what it means for their care. Set expectations now so you’re not having awkward payment conversations later.

Insurance will always be unpredictable, but your business strategy doesn’t have to be.
