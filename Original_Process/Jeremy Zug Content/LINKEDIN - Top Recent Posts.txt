Jefferson Health walked away from their Cigna contract on March 15th. 

As we watch more health systems walk away from contracts with major insurers, it makes you wonder when is the right time to enter a relationship with an insurance company. 

For you independent private practices, the decision to “go steady” with an insurance company is less about breakups than it is your practice thriving. 

But it can feel like deciding whether to adopt a particularly demanding houseplant. 

Diving into a network can mean a steady stream of referrals and revenue, which is great when you’re trying to avoid staring at an empty waiting room and wondering if your furniture is judging you. 

On the flip side, you might find yourself negotiating rates and navigating paperwork that would make a seasoned bureaucrat weep. 

So, when do you take the plunge?

Here are some ways you can think about when it’s time to jump in:

 1️⃣ Your Ideal Client: Are they mostly insurance-reliant? Or do they value out-of-pocket flexibility?

 2️⃣ Your Practice’s Stability: Can you handle the administrative load and potential rate reductions?

 3️⃣ Your Sanity: Is the potential stress worth the increased patient volume? (This one’s a deeply personal question)

Ultimately, it’s a balancing act. 

You want to get it right, but you also want to avoid a complete circus.

A “C”

That’s where we are with the U.S. healthcare system. 

A recent survey gave it an average grade of C, with patients calling it expensive, confusing, and downright frustrating. (Shocking, I know.)

For providers, this means dealing with insurance hurdles, surprise claim denials, and patients who are just as lost as you are. 

And yet, you’re still expected to run a profitable practice while navigating this mess.

So, what’s the move? 

Since fixing the whole system is slightly outside your job description, here are three ways to keep your practice financially strong despite the chaos:

1️⃣ Know your payer contracts inside and out. Insurance companies aren’t in the business of making sure you get paid. Review your rates, timelines, and appeals process before a claim gets denied.

2️⃣ Make cost conversations normal. Patients are drowning in out-of-pocket costs. Clear financial policies, transparent pricing, and benefits verification can help prevent sticker shock.

3️⃣ Get proactive about collections. A “wait and see” approach to patient balances is how you end up with thousands in unpaid claims. Automated reminders and upfront payments make a huge difference.

The healthcare system might be stuck at a C, but your practice doesn’t have to be.

Trying to get paneled with insurance companies can feel like trying to convince a cat to take a bath. 

Here are a few common reasons why your private practice's credentialing application might be stuck in the bureaucratic abyss:

1️⃣ The Missing Link: Did you dot every "i" and cross every "t"? Insurance companies are sticklers for details. Missing or incomplete information is a surefire way to get your application tossed back. Treat it like a highly classified document. 

2️⃣ The Expired Doc: Are all your documents up-to-date and valid? Expired licenses, outdated certifications – these are red flags waving furiously. Make sure your paperwork isn't older than your favorite pair of comfy socks. 

3️⃣ The Location Labyrinth: Sometimes you need a document but can’t find it. Keeping all your documents in an organized file structure really speeds up the process. 

Getting credentialed is a marathon, not a sprint. 

Paying attention to these often-overlooked details can save you a world of frustration (and payment delays).
