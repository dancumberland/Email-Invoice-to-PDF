# AI Guide: Defining Linguistic Patterns

**Objective for AI:** Your primary goal is to analyze the provided client materials (e.g., existing successful content, brand guides, style guides, ICP definitions, competitor content analysis provided by the client) to define and document the client's specific linguistic patterns. This includes preferred vocabulary, sentence structures, rhetorical devices, and overall cadence, ensuring consistent and on-brand communication.

**Guiding Principles for AI:**
*   **Contextual Analysis:** Understand how linguistic patterns might vary (if at all) based on platform, audience (referencing specific ICPs), and content purpose (e.g., educational, persuasive, entertaining).
*   **Prescriptive & Descriptive:** Document existing strong patterns observed in successful client content (descriptive) and, where appropriate or if client materials suggest, prescribe desired patterns for future content (prescriptive).
*   **Practical Examples:** Provide clear "do" and "don't" examples. These examples should ideally be extracted or synthesized from client materials or be highly relevant to their domain.
*   **Clarity & Actionability:** The resulting guide must be easy for human writers and other AI content generators to understand and apply effectively.
*   **Identify Gaps:** If client materials lack clarity on certain linguistic aspects, note this as an area for the client to consider or provide further input on.

## Linguistic Pattern Definition Process:

*AI Task: For each section below, meticulously review all provided client materials. Synthesize your findings into clear, actionable guidelines, supported by specific examples.*

### 1. Vocabulary & Lexicon (The Words We Use)

**A. Preferred Terminology & Industry Jargon:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Analyze client materials (especially industry-specific content, product/service descriptions, internal communications, and glossaries if provided) to identify frequently used and preferred technical terms, industry jargon, and brand-specific nomenclature.
    *   *List these key terms.*
    *   *Explain their meaning in the client's context.*
    *   *Provide guidelines on when and how they should be used (e.g., target audience, level of detail).*
    *   *Are there terms that are core to the client's unique selling proposition or identity? Highlight these.*
*   **AI Prompt:** Does the client use specific acronyms or abbreviations?
    *   *List them and provide guidelines for their usage (e.g., spell out on first use, always use in specific contexts).*

**B. Words & Phrases to Embrace (Brand Resonators):**
*   **AI Prompt:** Identify words and phrases that strongly align with the client's brand personality (as defined in `01_Core_Brand_Identity/01_Brand_Foundation.md`), values, and desired overall tone (e.g., innovative, empathetic, authoritative, playful).
    *   *Provide a list of 10-20 "power words" or resonant phrases that exemplify the brand's voice.*
    *   *Give 2-3 examples for each, showing their use in context, ideally drawn from or inspired by client materials.*

**C. Words & Phrases to Avoid (Brand Dissonance):**
*   **AI Prompt:** Based on client materials, stated preferences, or analysis of off-brand content, identify words, phrases, or types of jargon that should be avoided.
    *   *Categories to consider: overly complex/academic language (unless ICP dictates), outdated terms, clichés, hype words, jargon unfamiliar to the target audience, terms used heavily by competitors (if differentiation is key), or words that contradict brand values (e.g., aggressive language for an empathetic brand).*
    *   *List specific words/phrases to avoid and explain why (e.g., "avoid 'synergy' as it feels corporate and outdated for our startup audience").*
    *   *Provide examples of what to use instead, if applicable.*
*   **AI Prompt:** Are there any "buzzwords" the client explicitly dislikes or wishes to use sparingly? List them.

**D. Formality, Colloquialisms & Inclusivity:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Analyze the general level of formality in the client's most successful and on-brand content.
    *   *Is it typically formal, semi-formal, informal, or highly conversational?*
    *   *Are colloquialisms, slang, or idioms appropriate? If so, in what contexts and for which ICPs? Provide examples of acceptable and unacceptable usage.*
*   **AI Prompt:** Analyze client materials for evidence of or stated commitment to inclusive language.
    *   *Provide guidelines on using gender-neutral terms, person-first language, avoiding stereotypes, and ensuring accessibility in language choices. Point to any client-provided resources on DEI (Diversity, Equity, Inclusion).*

### 2. Sentence Structure & Syntax (How We Build Sentences)

**A. Preferred Sentence Length & Complexity:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Analyze sentence length patterns in the client's best-performing content.
    *   *Are sentences typically short and punchy (e.g., average 10-15 words), medium length and informative (e.g., 15-25 words), or occasionally long and elaborate (e.g., 25+ words for specific effects)?*
    *   *Is there a preferred mix or variation in sentence length to maintain reader engagement? Describe this pattern.*
    *   *Provide guidelines and examples of sentences that fit the preferred length and style, and examples of those that don't.*
*   **AI Prompt:** Assess the typical complexity of sentence structures.
    *   *Does the client favor simple sentences, compound sentences, complex sentences, or a mix?*
    *   *Provide examples of good sentence structure aligned with the brand voice and examples of overly convoluted or simplistic structures to avoid.*

**B. Voice (Active vs. Passive):**
*   **AI Prompt:** Determine the predominant grammatical voice used in on-brand client content.
    *   *Is active voice strongly preferred (e.g., for clarity, directness, energy)? Provide a clear guideline (e.g., "Use active voice 90% of the time").*
    *   *Are there any specific, acceptable instances for passive voice (e.g., when the agent is unknown or unimportant, for stylistic variation in certain contexts)? If so, define these sparingly.*
    *   *Illustrate with clear examples: "We launched the product" (Active) vs. "The product was launched by us" (Passive). Show how to convert passive to active.*

**C. Punctuation & Grammar Style:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Identify any specific punctuation styles or grammatical preferences evident in client materials or style guides.
    *   *Examples: Use of the Oxford comma, preferences for contractions (e.g., "it's" vs. "it is"), use of em-dashes vs. hyphens, capitalization in titles/headings.*
    *   *Document these specific preferences for consistency.*
    *   *If no strong pattern or client style guide exists, recommend adherence to a standard style guide (e.g., AP Stylebook, Chicago Manual of Style - client to specify if preferred) or default to common best practices for clarity and readability.*

### 3. Rhetorical Devices & Persuasive Language (Adding Impact & Flair)

**A. Commonly Used/Effective Rhetorical Devices:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Analyze client content (especially marketing copy, speeches, key presentations) for recurring or particularly effective use of rhetorical devices.
    *   *Examples: metaphors, similes, analogies, storytelling snippets, rhetorical questions, repetition for emphasis, parallelism for rhythm, alliteration, humor, irony, understatement, hyperbole (if appropriate for the brand).*
    *   *List 3-5 devices that are characteristic of or highly effective for the client's brand.*
    *   *For each, provide 1-2 examples of its use in context and explain why it's effective for engaging their target ICPs.*
*   **AI Prompt:** Which devices seem most resonant with the client's brand personality and audience? Which should be actively encouraged in new content creation?

**B. Persuasion Techniques & Calls to Action (CTAs):**
*   **AI Prompt:** Identify any patterns in how the client persuades their audience or frames their calls to action.
    *   *What persuasive appeals are common (e.g., appeals to logic/reason, emotion, ethics/authority, social proof, scarcity, urgency, benefit-driven language)?*
    *   *How are these techniques woven into their language? Provide examples.*
    *   *Analyze the structure and language of effective CTAs used by the client. Are they direct, benefit-oriented, urgent? List common CTA phrases.*

### 4. Cadence, Rhythm & Flow (The Music of Our Words)

**A. Pacing & Rhythm:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Describe the overall cadence and rhythm of the client's language when it's most effective.
    *   *Is it generally fast-paced and energetic, or calm, measured, and thoughtful? Does it vary by content type or ICP?*
    *   *How is this rhythm achieved (e.g., through sentence length variation, word choice – monosyllabic vs. polysyllabic, punctuation, use of short paragraphs, parallelism)?*
    *   *Provide 1-2 text passages from client materials that exemplify the desired rhythm and explain why they work.*

**B. Flow & Transitions:**
*   **AI Prompt:** Analyze how the client typically ensures smooth and logical transitions between ideas, sentences, and paragraphs.
    *   *Are specific transitional words or phrases commonly used (e.g., "Furthermore," "However," "In contrast," "As a result," "Next," "Meanwhile")? List common ones.*
    *   *Are there other techniques used to create flow (e.g., repeating a key word or concept, using pronouns effectively, structuring arguments logically)?*
    *   *Provide examples of effective transitions from client content.*
