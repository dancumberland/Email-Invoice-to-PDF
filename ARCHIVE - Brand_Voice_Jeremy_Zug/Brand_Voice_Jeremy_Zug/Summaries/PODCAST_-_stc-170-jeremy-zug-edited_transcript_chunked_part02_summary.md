### Summary:
This second and final chunk of "Selling the Couch" (Session 170) continues with Jeremy Zug advising host Melvin Varghese on how therapists can address rejections from "closed" insurance panels.

Strategies to appeal a closed panel rejection:
1.  **Show Evidence-Based Outcomes:** Provide quantifiable data (from EHRs, progress notes) demonstrating successful client treatment using evidence-based modalities. This is highly valued by insurers.
2.  **Demonstrate Client Demand:** Present data on the number of inquiries received from potential clients who are members of that specific insurance panel.
3.  **Highlight Existing Self-Pay Members:** Show the number of current self-pay clients who are members of that panel, illustrating they are paying higher out-of-pocket costs.
4.  **Rate Negotiation:** As a final option, cautiously offer a lower reimbursement rate to incentivize the insurer, while ensuring practice profitability.

Jeremy emphasizes that therapists have agency even after an initial rejection.

The discussion then covers the **top three mistakes clinicians make in paneling applications**:
1.  **Misinformation:** Errors in application details (e.g., incorrect address, NPI digits).
2.  **Prematurely Seeing Patients:** Misinterpreting CAQH notifications (e.g., "Payer X has added you to their roster") as confirmation of being in-network before official approval.
3.  **Over-Disclosure:** Sharing unnecessary sensitive information, like specific reimbursement rates from other insurers, which can make the applicant seem like a liability.

Jeremy invites listeners to contact Practice Solutions (practicesol.com) for assistance. The podcast concludes with Melvin summarizing key takeaways: the impact of specialization on paneling and the empowering strategies for appealing rejections.
