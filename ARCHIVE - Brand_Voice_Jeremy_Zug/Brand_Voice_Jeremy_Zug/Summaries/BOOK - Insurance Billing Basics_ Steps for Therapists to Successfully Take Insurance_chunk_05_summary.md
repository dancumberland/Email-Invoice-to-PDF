## 1. Summary Overview:
This fifth chunk of "Insurance Billing Basics" covers the entirety of **Chapter 5: Establishing Patient Relationships**. After setting up the practice's infrastructure for billing (Chapter 4), this chapter focuses on the crucial initial interactions with patients, covering patient acquisition and the first two stages of Revenue Cycle Management (RCM): Pre-Registration and Registration.

The chapter emphasizes that successful billing and a healthy practice depend heavily on well-organized initial patient interactions. Key sections include:

1.  **Create Your Patient Paperwork Documents:** Before seeing patients, essential documents must be prepared:
    *   HIPAA Consent Form
    *   Patient Intake Paperwork (collecting demographics)
    *   Practice’s Financial Policy (addressing payment on file, timing, cancellation fees, patient responsibility, rates, sliding scale/hardship policies)
    *   Payment Collection Form
    *   Optional: Release of Information form.

2.  **How to Acquire Patients:** While not the book's primary focus, it offers tips:
    *   **Utilize Resources from Your Insurance Partners:** Get listed on insurer provider registries and personalize your profile.
    *   **Expand Your Online Presence:** Maintain an updated, high-quality website reflecting your brand and mission. Consider directories like Psychology Today.
    *   **Build Customer Convenience:** Offer patient portals, online scheduling, and consider office hours outside normal business times. Smart hiring for billing staff also contributes to a positive patient experience.

3.  **RCM Stage 1: Pre-Registration:** This occurs *before* the patient's first office visit (e.g., after an online inquiry or phone call). It involves:
    *   Collecting extensive information for both therapeutic and billing purposes. A detailed list is provided, including:
        *   Patient & Subscriber Full Legal Name, DOB, SSN (subscriber), Address
        *   Insurance Provider(s) Name, ID, Group #
        *   Copies of Insurance Card (front/back) & Driver's License/ID (patient & subscriber)
        *   Treatment sought
        *   Signed HIPAA Consent, Financial Policy, and Payment Information Form (ideally digitally).
    *   The importance of confirming spelling and details against the insurance card.
    *   Designing a pre-registration process with clear roles, tools (forms, portals, HIPAA-compliant email), scripts, and scheduling methods.
    *   The goal is to gather as much accurate information as possible to facilitate smooth billing and eligibility checks.

4.  **RCM Stage 2: Registration or Patient Intake Process:** This occurs when the patient is *physically present* in the office for their first appointment. It involves:
    *   Verifying all information collected during pre-registration.
    *   Collecting any missing information or forms (HIPAA consent, financial policy, payment info, insurance card copies).
    *   Ensuring all data is accurately entered into the EHR system, double-checking against the insurance card.
    *   Confirming if an eligibility and benefit check was completed; if not, it must be done during this phase, with the expectation that the patient will be charged before leaving.
    *   Utilizing EHR patient portals or fillable/paper documents for information collection, always verifying EHR entries.
    *   Emphasizes that accuracy and precision in this stage are critical to prevent claim errors.

The chapter concludes with a **Chapter Summary** reiterating the importance of these initial stages for practice health and billing ease, followed by **Follow-Up Actions to Take**, such as developing patient acquisition strategies, creating checklists for pre-registration/registration, preparing paperwork, and establishing EHR data entry processes.
