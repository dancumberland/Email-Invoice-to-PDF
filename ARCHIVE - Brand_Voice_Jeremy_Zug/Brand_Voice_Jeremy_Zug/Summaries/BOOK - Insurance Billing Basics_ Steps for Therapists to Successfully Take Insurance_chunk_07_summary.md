# Summary of "BOOK - Insurance Billing Basics_chunk_07" (Lines 1265-1399)

## 1. Summary Overview:
This seventh chunk of "Insurance Billing Basics" covers the entirety of **Chapter 7: Completing Sessions**. It bridges the gap from pre-session administrative tasks to the actual therapy session and its subsequent documentation and billing requirements. The chapter focuses on three core areas: Charge Capture (RCM Stage 3), Coding for Billing, and Completing Notes.

Key sections include:

1.  **RCM Stage 3: Charge Capture (Lines 1269-1294):**
    *   **Definition:** Collecting payment from the patient, a critical revenue source and compliance practice.
    *   **Process:** Use E&B information to collect the correct amount (deductible, copay, coinsurance, or $0 if fully covered) before or after the session.
    *   **Payment Methods:** Credit card processors (often in EHR), cash, check, or other digital forms if a card declines.
    *   **Importance of Timely E&B:** Stresses collecting correct payment at time of service to avoid drastically reduced chances of receiving payment later.
    *   **Credit Card on File:** Can be a last resort, with patient notification and authorization for transactions.
    *   **Recording Payments:** Essential to record in EHR (or externally for later EHR entry), noting patient name, amount, payment type (deductible, copay, coinsurance), method, and date of service.
    *   **Self-Pay/Private Pay:** For uninsured, underinsured, or out-of-network patients, collect cash rate. Discuss superbill options for OON benefits.
    *   **Sliding Scale Fees:** Tailoring cash rates based on income (requires proof of hardship form).
    *   **Addressing Therapist Discomfort:** Acknowledges difficulty in requiring payment but emphasizes contractual obligations, cash flow, patient expectations, and valuing services.
    *   **Clear Communication:** Importance of a clear, fair billing policy communicated upfront.

2.  **Coding for Billing (Lines 1296-1372):**
    *   **Definition:** Translating services provided into standardized **Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) Codes** designated by the AMA.
    *   **Provider Responsibility:** Accurately document services in EHR using CPT codes for billing, reimbursement, and tracking treatment trends.
    *   **How to Code (Steps in EHR):
        1.  Identify appropriate code(s).
        2.  Enter code(s) into EHR.
        3.  Document session length.
        4.  Record codes for additional services (e.g., medication management).
        5.  Add necessary **modifiers** (e.g., 95 or GT for telehealth, HJ for EAP, license type modifiers – check contracts).
        6.  Save, submit, and verify.
    *   **Common CPT Codes (Not exhaustive):
        *   **Individual Therapy:** 90834 (45 min), 90837 (60 min), 90847 (family with patient, 45-50 min).
        *   **Group Therapy:** 90853 (60 min).
        *   **Assessments/Evaluations:** 90791 (Psychiatric Diagnostic Evaluation - intake), 90792 (Psychiatric Diagnostic Evaluation with medical services).
        *   **Psychological Testing:** 96136 (first 30 min admin/scoring), +96137 (each additional 30 min); 96130 (first hour eval/feedback), +96131 (each additional hour).
        *   **Neuropsychological Testing:** 96136 (first 30 min admin/scoring), +96137 (each additional 30 min); 96132 (first hour eval/feedback), +96132 (each additional hour - *note: book lists +96132 twice, likely a typo for +96133 or similar for additional neuro hours*).
        *   Others listed: 90791 (60 min eval), 90832 (30 min psychotherapy), 90846 (family conjoint with patient), 90853 (group), 90875/90876 (interactive complexity), 90887 (coordination of care).
    *   **Documentation Requirements:** Codes may have specific documentation needs and are subject to medical necessity review.

3.  **Completing Notes (Lines 1374-1388):**
    *   **Progress Note:** A concise, structured record of session content, interventions, and progress. Essential for tracking therapy, assessing effectiveness, ensuring care continuity, and meeting ethical/legal standards.
    *   **Content:** Diagnosis (Dx code), procedure code (CPT Code), date, start/end time, session summary, assessments/observations, patient response, future plan.
    *   **Requirement for Billing:** A claim cannot be billed without a completed progress note (with CPT & Dx).
    *   **Strategies for Timely Completion (Biggest barrier for timely filing):
        *   Schedule dedicated time blocks.
        *   View notes as potential paychecks (motivation).
        *   Complete oldest notes first.
        *   Group practice incentives/deadlines.
        *   Goal setting (e.g., "Write 5 Notes per day," share goals).

4.  **Chapter Summary (Lines 1390-1392):** Reinforces learning about coding best practices and note completion, critical as many EHRs block claim submission without notes.

5.  **Follow-Up Actions to Take (Lines 1394-1398):** Set daily time for notes, ensure coding accuracy, align codes with services performed.
