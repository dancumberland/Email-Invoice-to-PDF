# Summary of "BOOK - Insurance Billing Basics_chunk_10" (Lines 1746-1853)

## 1. Summary Overview:
This tenth chunk of "Insurance Billing Basics" covers the entirety of **Chapter 10: RCM Stage 6: Claim Follow-Up**. This stage is about identifying, investigating, and resolving issues with submitted claims that haven't been paid, including denials and rejections, to ensure accurate reimbursement and identify systemic billing problems.

Key sections include:

1.  **Identify Which Claims Require Follow-Up (Lines 1752-1766):**
    *   **Sources:** EOBs/ERAs (for denials, incorrect processing), EHR rejection notices, Insurance Aging Reports (summary & detail).
    *   **Focus:** Denials, rejections, unposted payments, incorrectly processed claims (vs. benefits/fee schedule), sessions without progress notes (unbilled claims).
    *   **Timing:** Generally, claims <45 days old don't need follow-up unless a rejection/denial is received. Start with oldest, largest aging amounts.
    *   **Action:** Compile a detailed list of claims needing follow-up (DOS, patient info, provider info, denial/rejection details, discovery date).

2.  **Decide the Best Course of Action for Each Open Date of Service (Lines 1768-1786):**
    *   **Post a Payment:** If an EOB/ERA is correct and simply needs posting (ideally done before follow-up).
    *   **Investigate EOB/ERA Discrepancies:** If disagreeing with allowed amount/patient responsibility, note details and call the insurer, recording reference numbers.
    *   **Complete Progress Notes:** For unbilled sessions within timely filing; prioritize oldest.
    *   **Resolve the Rejection or Denial:** More complex; detailed below.

3.  **How to Resolve a Rejection (Lines 1788-1804):**
    *   **Definition:** Claim rejected by the third-party clearinghouse due to demographic or insurance errors *before* reaching the insurance company.
    *   **Understand Rejection Reason:** Check EHR for rejection code/reason. If unclear, call payer's **EDI department** (not claims, as claim isn't in their adjudication system).
    *   **Common Reasons:** Incorrect patient DOB, invalid insurance (e.g., wrong ID), duplicate DOS, incorrect patient name.
    *   **Correct and Resubmit:** Refer to patient intake documents or verify provider credentialing. Fix error in EHR and resubmit. Ensure it doesn't reject again.

4.  **How to Resolve a Denial (Lines 1806-1826):**
    *   **Definition:** Claim denied by the insurance company *after* processing.
    *   **Understand Denial Reason:** Review EOB/ERA. If unclear, call payer's **claims department**.
    *   **Common Reasons:** Issues with diagnosis, treatment plan, patient demographics, insurance problems, missing modifiers, incorrect provider credentials, coverage lapse, duplicate submission.
    *   **Submit a Corrected Claim (Amended Claim):** For obvious errors (e.g., wrong modifier, patient ID). This indicates it's replacing the denied claim.
    *   **Continuing Denials:**
        *   If rightfully denied: Explain to patient (who owes the balance), inform about appeal options.
        *   If provider disagrees/repeatedly denied: Appeal the denial (submit more documentation, request reconsideration). Track all communication. If appeal fails, patient owes the balance; inform them early.

5.  **The Importance of Claim Follow-Up (Lines 1828-1838):**
    *   Ensures collection of all owed revenue.
    *   Identifies patterns of errors within the RCM system (e.g., problems with E&B check process, credentialing errors, provider note completion issues), allowing for systemic improvements.

6.  **Chapter Summary & Follow-Up Actions (Lines 1840-1850):**
    *   Claim issues are inevitable; consistent follow-up is crucial but time-consuming.
    *   **Actions:** Schedule time for follow-up (or reviewing biller's work), promptly get info from patients for resolution, maintain clear follow-up records, communicate clearly with patients about claim issues affecting them.
