# Summary of "BOOK - Insurance Billing Basics_chunk_11" (Lines 1854-1927)

## 1. Summary Overview:
This eleventh chunk of "Insurance Billing Basics" covers the entirety of **Chapter 11: RCM Stage 7: Outstanding Patient Collections**. This final RCM stage addresses following up on unpaid patient balances, which can accumulate despite best efforts due to issues like incorrect E&B checks or claims processing differently than anticipated.

Key sections include:

1.  **Introduction (Lines 1856-1858):**
    *   Acknowledges that collecting money can be uncomfortable for mental health practitioners.
    *   Stresses the importance of clear payment policies and effective communication to avoid conflicts.

2.  **What to Collect (Lines 1860-1866):**
    *   Use EOB/ERA to determine patient responsibility (verify against E&B, but EOB/ERA is more definitive).
    *   Reference patient aging reports to identify open balances.
    *   For private pay, charge the established cash rate or agreed-upon sliding scale.

3.  **How to Collect (Lines 1868-1912):**
    *   **Catch Up With a Patient the Next Time They are In-Office (Lines 1872-1878):**
        *   Check balances and discuss when the patient is present.
        *   Be prepared to explain balances using E&B, EOB/ERA, or plan details.
        *   Consider customized payment plans.
        *   Critical to collect before the patient leaves, as collection chances drop significantly afterward.
    *   **Set Clear Expectations (Lines 1880-1886):**
        *   Communicate policies regarding outstanding payments during intake.
        *   Reiterate expectations to prevent drift from the original agreement.
        *   Clear financial expectations can lead to a clean patient aging report and avoid collections agencies.
    *   **Always Keep a Card on File (Lines 1888-1894):**
        *   Recommended even if other payment methods are used.
        *   Provides control over cash flow (e.g., for missed/late sessions).
        *   Offers convenience and peace of mind for patients, allowing them to focus on therapy.
        *   Described as a "win-win scenario."
    *   **Sending Statements (Lines 1896-1906):**
        *   **Digital Reminders:** Email/text with payment links; cost-effective and encourages easy payment.
        *   **Paper Statements:** Start with normal paper. If no response, use colored paper (e.g., red for urgency) or stamps ("Outstanding Balance," "Final Notice") to get attention.
        *   Ensure clear payment instructions and multiple payment options (mail check, call with card, online portal).
    *   **Hire Someone to Collect Patient Balances for You (Lines 1908-1912):**
        *   If uncomfortable with direct collections, hire a third party (e.g., Practice Solutions) for sending statements and taking payment calls.
        *   As a final step for former patients with balances, consider a collections agency.

4.  **Chapter Summary & Follow-Up Actions (Lines 1914-1925):**
    *   Reiterates the discomfort of collections but emphasizes patient value for services.
    *   **Actions:** Discuss patient responsibility, always collect a card, charge cards after each session, send statements for unpaid balances, and hire a collections agency for problem patients if necessary.
