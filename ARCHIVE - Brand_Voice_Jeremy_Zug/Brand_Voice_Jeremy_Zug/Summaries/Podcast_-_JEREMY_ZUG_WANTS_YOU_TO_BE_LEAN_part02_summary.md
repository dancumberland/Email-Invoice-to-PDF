**Podcast:** Practice of the Practice with Joe Sanok – Session Number 297
**Guest:** Jeremy Zug, Co-owner of Practice Solutions

### Summary:
This segment of the podcast delves into the core concepts and practical application of LEAN methodology for private practice owners.

**Key Discussion Points:**

1.  **Core LEAN Concepts & Stages:**
    *   Jeremy outlines **LEAN as a 5-stage process** applicable to any organization:
        1.  **Define Value:** From the patient's (customer's) perspective.
        2.  **Map the Value Stream:** Identify key processes, issues, and constraints within them.
        3.  **Remove Wastes:** Eliminate non-value-adding activities.
        4.  **Implement Solutions:** Address the identified constraints and issues.
        5.  **Measure & Continually Improve:** Monitor effectiveness and make ongoing adjustments.
    *   Joe Sanok relates this to the **"Plan, Do, Check, Adjust" (PDCA)** cycle, which Jeremy confirms is fundamental to LEAN, emphasizing iterative improvement.
    *   Jeremy stresses that LEAN implementation can be quick (e.g., a 4-day intensive can yield significant results), focusing on rapid cycles of analysis, change, and measurement.

2.  **Applying LEAN in a Private Practice (A 3-4 Day Intensive):**
    *   **Preparation is Key:**
        *   Gather materials: Whiteboard, large paper, markers, sticky notes.
        *   **Crucially, pre-identify 5-8 key business processes** (e.g., patient experience, marketing, billing, finance/APAR).
    *   **The Intensive Process:**
        1.  **Map Processes Granularly:** Detail each step of the identified key business processes.
        2.  **Identify Patient Value:** Define what patients truly value, categorizing into:
            *   **Expected (Unspoken):** Correct billing, warm office, prompt service.
            *   **Expected (Asked For):** Convenient location, reminders, Wi-Fi.
            *   **"Wow" Factors (Delighters):** Online payments, auto-pay, easy appointment booking.
        3.  **Time Value Analysis:** Assign a time duration to each step in a process to quantify effort and identify bottlenecks or areas for efficiency gains (e.g., time spent on eligibility checks).
        4.  **Conduct Value-Add Analysis:** Determine which activities provide meaningful value. For a process to add value in healthcare, Jeremy states one key criterion:
            *   **The patient has to care about it** (e.g., a clear, useful website).

This part emphasizes a structured, patient-centric approach to LEAN, focusing on defining value and mapping processes before moving to waste elimination.
