## Summary
This LinkedIn post uses a cautionary tale of a healthcare provider committing Medicare fraud to emphasize the importance of treating third-party payers as crucial business partners. It advises private practices to strategically manage their relationships with insurance companies by: choosing payers wisely, understanding and adhering to compliance rules, setting rates based on practice profitability rather than just payer allowances, building robust revenue cycle systems instead of relying on ad-hoc processes, and proactively auditing billing practices to avoid issues. The post concludes by inviting readers to share their biggest lessons in working with insurance.

*   **Direct/Advisory:** "So, how do you make insurance work for your practice instead of against it? A few strategies:"
*   **Slightly Sarcastic/Wry (referring to difficult payers):** "...others hold your money hostage like a villain in a bad action movie."
*   **Emphasis on Proactivity:** "Audit Before They Do – If your billing practices can’t stand up to scrutiny, assume someone will eventually scrutinize them."
*   **Consequential Tone:** "The alternative? You might find out what your business looks like when your “partner” calls in federal investigators instead of sending a reimbursement check."

## Notable Quotes
*   "If you bill insurance, third-party payers are your business partners—whether you like it or not."
*   "Compliance isn’t just about avoiding jail time—it’s about getting paid consistently."
*   "Charge What You’re Worth – If you underprice yourself just to take insurance, you’ll resent every claim you file."
*   "Build Systems, Not Bottlenecks – If your revenue cycle depends on one person “figuring it out” as they go, you’re asking for denials, delays, and a daily dose of financial anxiety."
*   "If insurance is part of your business model, treat it like a relationship worth managing—not a necessary evil."
