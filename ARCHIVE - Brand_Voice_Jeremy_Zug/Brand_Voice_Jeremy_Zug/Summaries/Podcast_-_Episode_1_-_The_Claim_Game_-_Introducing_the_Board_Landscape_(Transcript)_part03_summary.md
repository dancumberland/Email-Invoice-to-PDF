### Summary:
This final chunk of "The Claim Game" Episode 1 sees Jeremy and Kathryn Zug conclude their overview of the Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) "Game Board."

**5. Aging & Follow-up (Territory 5 - Continued):
*   **Patient Aging:** The hosts clarify that "Aging & Follow-up" includes managing outstanding balances from patients, not just insurance companies. This requires distinct processes.
*   **Collections Process:**
    *   **Soft Collections:** A structured approach for recent patient balances (e.g., emails, letters, calls based on balance age).
    *   **Hard Collections:** A more assertive strategy for significantly overdue accounts (e.g., 90+ days).
    *   The importance of robust patient registration, clear checkout procedures, payment plan policies, and financial hardship policies is stressed to minimize aggressive collection needs.
*   **Monitoring:** Both insurance and patient aging reports (especially balances >90 days) should show a downward trend over time, indicating effective RCM.

**6. Claim Submission (Territory 6 - The Final Step in the Cycle):
*   **Prerequisites:** A claim is only ready for submission after the clinical note is completed and signed by the provider. Accurate data checking (patient info, procedure codes, rates, modifiers, prior authorizations) is vital before submission.
*   **Note Completion:** Acknowledged as a common challenge for providers, but essential for compliance and timely payment.
*   **Submission Frequency:** While daily submission isn't always advantageous due to inconsistent payer payment schedules, having a defined internal process (daily, weekly, or bi-weekly) is key for setting expectations for note completion and claim review, creating rhythm and accountability.
*   **Rejections vs. Denials:** A critical distinction is made:
    *   **Rejection:** Occurs when a claim is sent back by the **Clearinghouse** (an intermediary between EHR and payer) due to data errors. These are typically flagged in the EHR and are generally simpler to fix as they haven't reached the payer.
    *   **Denial:** Occurs when the **Payer (Insurance Company)** processes a claim and finds issues (e.g., with credentials, benefits, medical necessity). Denials are communicated via EOBs/ERAs and are usually more complex to resolve.

**Episode Wrap-up & Practice Solutions Background:**
*   **RCM Board Recap:** The six territories (Credentialing, Patient Registration, E&B, Payment Posting, Aging & Follow-up, Claim Submission) are summarized, with the latter four forming the operational RCM cycle.
*   **Future Content:** A list of RCM terminology will be available in show notes. They introduce "Case Study Cards" as a recurring segment to analyze real-life RCM scenarios.
*   **Practice Solutions Origin:** Jeremy and Kathryn share their journey, starting in 2016 when asked to help with billing for Jeremy's mother's colleagues. They officially launched Practice Solutions in March 2017.
*   **Mission:** To alleviate the burden of billing and educate providers on RCM, enabling them to focus on patient care. This podcast is a key part of their educational mission.
