# Summary of "BOOK - Insurance Billing Basics_chunk_01" (Lines 0-263)

## 1. Summary Overview:
This initial chunk of "Insurance Billing Basics" serves as a comprehensive introduction and lays the foundational groundwork for mental health clinicians looking to understand and navigate the complexities of insurance billing. It begins by identifying its target audience: new practitioners, those starting private practices, or those struggling with current billing processes. The authors, Jeremy Zug, Kathryn Zug, and Kate Perry from Practice Solutions, state their mission to empower providers by demystifying insurance billing.

The text assumes basic business setup (NPI, tax ID) and outlines the book's structure, emphasizing actionable steps at the end of each chapter.

The core of this chunk is **Chapter 1: The Basic Process of Working with Insurance**. This chapter is divided into two main sections:
    *   **Credentialing:** Defined as the process of becoming an "In-Network" provider. It details the application process (obtaining forms, required information like NPI/Tax ID, submission, and the typical 6-12 month processing time), the importance of contract review, and re-verification requirements (typically every three years). It also clarifies when new credentialing is needed (e.g., practice changes, moving states, group NPI changes) and briefly touches on Out-of-Network billing.
    *   **Billing (Revenue Cycle Management - RCM):** This section defines billing as submitting session information for reimbursement post-credentialing. It introduces the seven stages of RCM, which are managed via Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems:
        1.  **Pre-registration:** Initial patient contact, information gathering (patient/subscriber details, insurance info), and conducting an Eligibility & Benefits (E&B) check.
        2.  **Registration:** In-office verification of information, collecting signed forms (HIPAA, financial policy), and ensuring EHR accuracy. This is highlighted as a critical step for a "clean claim."
        3.  **Charge Capture:** Collecting patient payment (copay, deductible) at the time of service based on E&B check.
        4.  **Claim Submission:** Transmitting session information (via CMS-1500 paper form or EDI electronic claim) to the insurance company after completing therapy notes.
        5.  **Payment Posting:** Recording insurance payments based on Explanation of Benefits (EOB) or Electronic Remittance Advice (ERA), which detail how claims were processed or denied.
        6.  **Claim Follow-Up:** Resolving outstanding, rejected, or denied claims by working with the insurance company.
        7.  **Patient Collections:** Collecting any remaining patient balances.

The chapter then briefly discusses **The Ethics of Insurance Billing**, emphasizing the contractual responsibility to both insurance companies (timely, accurate claims) and patients (properly updated plans, accurate cost collection).

Finally, it introduces **Practice Solutions** as a potential revenue cycle management partner, outlining their educational resources (like this book), billing services, and Practice Health Checks.

Chapter 1 concludes with a **Chapter Summary** and actionable **Follow-Up Actions** for the reader, such as training staff, journaling about the desired customer experience, documenting information flow, and identifying knowledge gaps. It sets the stage for Chapter 2, which will focus on deciding which insurance payers to work with.
