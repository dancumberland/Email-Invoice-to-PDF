Picture this: You’ve built a thriving practice, patients trust you, and the work is meaningful. That is not what happened to one man. 

A healthcare provider who thought siphoning $17 million from Medicare through bogus hospice and home health claims was a solid business plan.

Turns out, the DOJ had a different interpretation.

Most of us aren’t running a multi-state fraud ring (hopefully), but there’s a lesson here: If you bill insurance, third-party payers are your business partners—whether you like it or not. And just like any partner, they have expectations, rules, and, inconveniently, the ability to say “no” to your claims.

So, how do you make insurance work for your practice instead of against it? A few strategies:

🔹 Pick Your Partners Wisely – Not all payers are created equal. Some reimburse quickly and fairly; others hold your money hostage like a villain in a bad action movie. Do your research before signing contracts.

🔹 Know the Rules, Follow the Rules – Just because a claim could go through doesn’t mean it should. Compliance isn’t just about avoiding jail time—it’s about getting paid consistently.

🔹 Charge What You’re Worth – If you underprice yourself just to take insurance, you’ll resent every claim you file. Set rates based on what keeps your practice profitable, not just what payers will give you.

🔹 Build Systems, Not Bottlenecks – If your revenue cycle depends on one person “figuring it out” as they go, you’re asking for denials, delays, and a daily dose of financial anxiety.

🔹 Audit Before They Do – If your billing practices can’t stand up to scrutiny, assume someone will eventually scrutinize them. Stay ahead of audits and payment reviews before they become existential threats.

If insurance is part of your business model, treat it like a relationship worth managing—not a necessary evil. 

The alternative? 

You might find out what your business looks like when your “partner” calls in federal investigators instead of sending a reimbursement check.
